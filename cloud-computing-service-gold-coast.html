The Problem

You Started on a Personal Account. Now You Have a Team. This Is the Moment.

A lot of Gold Coast small businesses start on Microsoft 365 Personal. It makes sense at the time — one person, one device, one subscription. But businesses grow. Staff are hired. Files need to be shared. And at some point the same login that worked for one person becomes the entire company’s Microsoft account. Everyone signs in with the owner’s credentials. OneDrive is a shared folder one accidental deletion away from a serious problem. There is no way to disable a former employee’s access. There is no admin control over who can see what.

That is not a Microsoft 365 problem — it is the right product being used for the wrong situation. Microsoft 365 Personal is designed for one person. Microsoft 365 Business is designed for a team. The migration from one to the other is not technically complex, but it needs to be planned carefully to avoid losing email history, breaking existing workflows or creating a period where your Gold Coast team is locked out of the tools they rely on.

Your whole team is sharing one Microsoft login.

When everyone uses the owner’s account, there is no audit trail, no individual accountability and no way to revoke access when someone leaves.

Files are in OneDrive but nobody knows whose OneDrive.

Consumer OneDrive is personal storage. When the account holder leaves — or forgets their password — the files go with them. SharePoint is where business files belong.

A staff member left and you’re not sure they can still access your business files.

Without individual accounts and admin controls, offboarding a staff member from your Microsoft 365 environment is not straightforward. With Microsoft 365 Business, it is a two-click operation.

Your staff email addresses don’t match your business domain.

Staff using gmail.com or @hotmail.com addresses for business email, or all sharing one info@yourdomain.com address, signals a business that hasn’t fully set up its platform. Every staff member should have their own firstname@yourdomain.com account.

If any of these sound familiar, the migration is simpler than you think. Call 07 3041 8993 to discuss what it involves for your Gold Coast business.

Why Migrations Need Planning

Email migrations that are not planned carefully can result in lost email history, duplicate accounts, broken distribution groups or a cutover period where staff cannot send or receive email. OneDrive migrations that skip the file structure planning stage often recreate the same disorganised folder chaos in the cloud that existed on the local drive. MFA rolled out without user preparation causes lockouts. None of these outcomes are inevitable — they are the result of migrating without a plan. How It Works

How a Cloud Migration Works for a Gold Coast Small Business

Every migration is different in detail but follows the same four stages. Here is what the process looks like.

01

We Document What You Have Now

Before anything is moved we map your current state — what accounts exist, where your files live, what your email setup looks like and what your team actually uses day to day. This is the stage most DIY migrations skip, and it is why they run into problems.

02

We Plan the Migration and Confirm the Sequence

We produce a migration plan specific to your Gold Coast business — what moves first, what the cutover window is, what your staff need to know beforehand and what the rollback position is if something unexpected happens. You approve the plan before anything changes.

03

Migration Executed — Tested Before Cutover

Files, email and account configurations are migrated and tested in a staging state before cutover. Staff accounts are prepared, MFA is enrolled and the new environment is confirmed working before the old one is switched off. Handover and Staff Briefing

After cutover we confirm everything is working, brief your staff on the new environment and hand over admin credentials with documentation. You know how to add a new user, reset a password and manage licences — or we handle that for you as part of an ongoing managed IT arrangement.
