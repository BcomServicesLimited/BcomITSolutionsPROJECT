IT Consulting in Practice: Cloud Migration for a Robina Medical Practice

A real example of what independent IT advice delivers for a Gold Coast business.

A Robina-based medical practice was spending approximately $3,200 per month on a legacy on-premise server that required constant maintenance, caused regular downtime, and was no longer meeting RACGP clinical records standards. The practice manager had received three vendor proposals ranging from $18,000 to $42,000, all recommending different solutions, and had no way to evaluate which was genuinely appropriate for their situation.

bcom ICT was engaged for an independent consulting review. Over two sessions at the practice, we assessed the existing infrastructure, reviewed the vendor proposals, and mapped the practice’s actual workflow requirements against the proposed solutions. Our recommendation was a phased migration to Microsoft 365 Business Premium combined with a RACGP-compliant cloud-hosted clinical management system — a solution none of the three vendors had proposed because it did not involve selling hardware.

The migration was completed over a weekend to avoid disrupting patient appointments. Monthly IT costs dropped from $3,200 to $890. The practice now meets RACGP digital health standards, staff can access records securely from any device, and the practice manager has a documented IT roadmap for the next three years.

Cyber Governance and ASIC Compliance as Part of IT Strategy

For many Gold Coast businesses, cybersecurity has historically been treated as a technical problem — something to hand off to IT support. Increasingly, it is a governance problem. Boards and senior management are expected to understand, oversee and take accountability for their organisation’s cyber risk posture. This shift is most acute for regulated businesses, but it applies to any organisation that holds client data, processes payments or relies on digital systems to operate.

For businesses regulated by ASIC — including financial planners, mortgage brokers, accountants providing financial advice, insurance brokers and investment advisers — this governance expectation is now explicit. ASIC’s open letter of 8 May 2026 (Media Release 26-092MR) identified six cyber resilience fundamentals that all AFS licensees and credit licensees are expected to implement. These include governance frameworks and board-level accountability, defence-in-depth architecture, patch management governance, privileged access management, incident response playbooks and supply chain risk management.

ASIC 26-092MR — May 2026

ASIC has formally identified six cyber resilience fundamentals that AFS licensees and regulated entities must address. These are not aspirational guidelines — they are the baseline expectations against which ASIC will assess whether a licensee has met its obligations under the Corporations Act 2001. bcom ICT delivers the full technical implementation of every one of these fundamentals for regulated businesses on the Gold Coast.

Integrating cyber governance into your IT strategy means more than installing security software. It means documenting your risk posture, establishing clear accountability at board level, implementing tested incident response procedures, and ensuring your technology decisions — including cloud migrations, software platform choices and vendor relationships — are assessed through a security lens. This is exactly the kind of strategic IT advice that bcom ICT provides as part of an IT consulting engagement.
