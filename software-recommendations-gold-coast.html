The Problem

Most Gold Coast Small Businesses Are Paying for Software They Do Not Need — or Missing Software They Do

Software subscriptions accumulate. A business signs up for a tool to solve a specific problem, solves it, and then keeps paying the monthly fee long after the problem is forgotten. Another team member signs up for a different tool that does the same thing. A third tool gets added during a trial period and never cancelled.

The result is a software stack that has grown organically rather than by design — overlapping tools, unused subscriptions and, sometimes, important gaps where a proper solution was never put in place. For a Gold Coast business of ten staff, it is not unusual for monthly software spend to be 30–40 percent higher than it needs to be while still missing something important.

You are paying for Dropbox and Microsoft 365 OneDrive. They do the same thing.

Businesses that move to Microsoft 365 often keep paying for Dropbox out of habit. If your files are in OneDrive, the Dropbox subscription is redundant.

You are not sure what software you are paying for this month.

If your software subscriptions are spread across multiple credit cards and owner accounts with no central record, the first step is a stack audit — find out what is running before deciding what to change.

Different staff are using different tools for the same task.

One person uses Teams for messaging, another uses WhatsApp, a third emails. The result is scattered conversations, missed information and no single record of what was decided or agreed.

You have no password manager — staff are using their browser or a spreadsheet.

A spreadsheet of passwords is not a password manager. A browser saving passwords does not enforce strong passwords, share them securely with the team or revoke access when someone leaves.

If any of these sound familiar, call 07 3041 8993 — a software stack review typically pays for itself within a month.

Productivity & Collaboration

Recommended: Microsoft 365 Business Standard

Microsoft 365 Business Standard is the recommended starting point for most Gold Coast small businesses — it includes Outlook, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Teams, OneDrive and SharePoint under a single per-user monthly licence. The Business Standard tier adds desktop app installs and SharePoint, which the Basic tier lacks. Google Workspace Business Starter is the right choice for businesses that have invested in Google and whose team already works well within that ecosystem. Avoid mixing the two platforms across the same team — split tooling creates collaboration and file compatibility problems.

Accounting & Finance

Recommended: Xero (most Gold Coast SMBs)

Xero is cloud-based, has strong Australian bank feed integration, is supported by the majority of Gold Coast accountants and bookkeepers and scales well from sole trader to medium business. MYOB Business is a reasonable alternative for businesses already established on MYOB or with an accountant who prefers it. QuickBooks is less common in the Australian market but a capable alternative. The platform matters less than having one — do not run your business accounts on spreadsheets.

Team Communication

Recommended: Microsoft Teams (M365 subscribers)

Microsoft Teams is included in every Microsoft 365 Business subscription and covers messaging, video calls, file sharing and channel-based team communication. For businesses already paying for Microsoft 365, using Teams costs nothing extra and removes the need for a separate messaging tool. Google Chat is the equivalent for Google Workspace subscribers. If your team is on neither platform, Slack is a capable standalone option — but adds a subscription on top of your existing productivity stack.

File Storage & Document Management

Recommended: OneDrive + SharePoint (M365) or Google Drive

For Microsoft 365 businesses: individual files go in OneDrive, shared business files go in SharePoint — not in someone’s personal OneDrive. For Google Workspace businesses: Google Drive with Shared Drives for team files. Businesses paying for Dropbox, Box or similar cloud storage in addition to an existing Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace subscription are almost always paying twice for the same capability. Consolidate onto the platform you are already using.

Password Management

Recommended: Bitwarden Business

Every Gold Coast business with more than one staff member needs a password manager. Bitwarden Business is open source, independently audited, affordable and straightforward to deploy across a team. 1Password Business is a well-regarded alternative with a strong track record. LastPass is not recommended following its 2022 security breach. Using a browser to save passwords is not a substitute — it does not enforce strong passwords, share credentials securely or revoke access when a staff member leaves.

Endpoint Security & Antivirus

Recommended: Microsoft Defender for Business (M365 Business Premium)

Microsoft Defender for Business — included in Microsoft 365 Business Premium — provides centralised endpoint detection, response and management across all staff devices. For businesses on lower Microsoft 365 tiers, Malwarebytes Business is a recommended supplement. Avoid free consumer antivirus products in a business environment — they lack central management and are not designed for team deployments. Free is not an appropriate security budget for a business.

Backup

Recommended: Third-party solution covering M365 + endpoints

Microsoft 365 is not a backup — it synchronises data and retains deleted items for a limited period, but does not provide point-in-time recovery. A third-party backup solution covering Exchange, OneDrive and SharePoint is essential for any Gold Coast business relying on Microsoft 365. Veeam Backup for Microsoft 365 and Backupify are both reliable options. For endpoint backup, Veeam Agent and Acronis Cyber Backup are recommended for Windows environments.

Remote Access & Support

Recommended: TeamViewer Business or AnyDesk Business

If your Gold Coast business uses remote access tools for any purpose — staff working from home, IT support access or connecting to office systems remotely — use a paid business-grade product with audit logging, access controls and secure authentication. Free and personal versions of TeamViewer, AnyDesk and similar tools are not appropriate in a business environment — they lack the controls needed to manage access safely and are frequently targeted in social engineering attacks.
