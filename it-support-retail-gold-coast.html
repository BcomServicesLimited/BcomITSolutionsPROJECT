IT Support for Gold Coast Retail Businesses

Gold Coast retail spans a wide range — large national chains in Pacific Fair, Robina Town Centre and Harbour Town at Biggera Waters, boutique independents across Burleigh Heads, Broadbeach and Mermaid Beach, surf and lifestyle stores throughout the beachside suburbs and specialty retailers in the suburban shopping centres from Coomera to Coolangatta. Every one of these businesses processes card payments, manages inventory through a cloud or on-premise system, and depends on reliable internet connectivity to do both.

The most common IT issue we resolve for Gold Coast retail businesses is POS and EFTPOS connectivity — terminals that stop processing because an IP address changed after a router reboot, or EFTPOS performance degraded because it shares a network segment with a busy customer WiFi connection. We configure the network correctly — EFTPOS and POS terminals on a stable, isolated VLAN separate from customer and staff WiFi — so these problems don’t occur in the first place.

Pacific Fair in Broadbeach, Robina Town Centre, Harbour Town at Biggera Waters and the smaller centres throughout the Gold Coast’s residential suburbs are where we do most of our retail IT work. We scale the solution to the business size.

Any business that accepts card payments has a PCI DSS obligation — specifically to ensure that cardholder data environments are isolated from untrusted networks including customer WiFi. For most small Gold Coast retailers, this means correctly configuring network segmentation between the EFTPOS terminal and the customer-facing WiFi network. This is not complex or expensive — it requires the right switch configuration and WiFi setup, which we implement as standard.

Gold Coast retailers operating across multiple locations — a Surfers Paradise store and a Robina outlet, for example — need consistent IT setups, centralised access to inventory data and a single IT provider managing all locations. The most common causes are: the terminal’s IP address changing after a router reboot (fix: assign a static IP or DHCP reservation), the terminal sharing a congested WiFi network with customer devices (fix: isolate EFTPOS on a wired or dedicated wireless network), or an NBN fault affecting internet connectivity.
